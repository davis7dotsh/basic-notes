# book notes: The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick)

short read, ~2 hrs. re-reading bc of the portal + maybe a SaaS for creators

## core rules
1. talk about their life, not your pitch
2. ask about specifics in the past, not generics or opinions about the future
3. talk less, listen more

## bad qs → good qs
- "would you use an app that..." → "when's the last time you dealt with X? walk me through it"
- "how much would you pay" → "how much does this problem cost you now? what have you tried?"
- "do you think it's a good plan" → literally never ask this

## signals
- compliments = noise. "that's so cool!" means nothing
- fluff (generics, hypotheticals, "I would/I usually") → anchor back to specifics
- commitment = currency: time, reputation, money. ask for something at the end of every convo (intro, pilot, LOI, pre-order)
- if they haven't searched for a solution already, the problem probably isn't that painful

## process
- keep convos casual, 10-15 min, no "can i interview you"
- take notes, review w/ team, update beliefs
- segment: who *specifically* has this problem most? start there

## applying it
- creators: don't ask "would you use a portal", ask "how did you track your last 3 brand deals' payment status"
  - answer from Elsa: spreadsheet + gmail search, missed a $2k payment for 60 days. → real pain
- brands: ask how they find creators today, what went wrong on the last campaign

## quotes
> "You aren't allowed to tell them what their problems are, and in return, they aren't allowed to tell you what to build."
> "Opinions are worthless."

## my takeaways
- i've been doing this wrong for every side project
- write the 3 big questions before every convo
- if nobody asks "when can i get it", that's the answer
